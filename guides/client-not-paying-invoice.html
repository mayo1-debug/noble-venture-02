Client not paying your invoice? What to do, step by step

Send written reminders in a fixed order, then claim the statutory interest and fixed compensation. Each step below links to a free guide with an excerpt.

What to do, in order

  1. Letter 1: friendly reminder (3 days after the due date)
  2. Letter 2: firm reminder (7 days after letter 1)
  3. Letter 3: formal notice (14 days after letter 1)
  4. Letter 4: final notice (21 days after letter 1)
  5. Letter 5: payment plan offer (when they say they cannot pay in full)

Before you write

  • Check the invoice: number, amount, due date and the client's legal name are right.
  • Check the payment terms you agreed. The due date counts from there.
  • Check that the money is not on its way: look at your bank account first.
  • Keep every message. Dates and wording matter if the claim goes further.

What you can claim after the due date

For business-to-business invoices in the EU and UK, the law adds interest and a fixed compensation. The EU minimum rate is 10,4% per year now, plus EUR 40 per invoice. See the EU rule by country and the UK rule. Private consumers are not covered by these rules.

Do not

  • Do not threaten steps you will not take.
  • Do not contact the client's staff or customers about the debt.
  • Do not skip the written steps. A clear paper trail comes first.

This guide is general information, not legal advice.
