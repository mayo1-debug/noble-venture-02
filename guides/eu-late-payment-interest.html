EU late payment interest: the rule for business invoices

When a business client in the EU pays late, you can claim statutory interest of at least the reference rate plus 8 points, and at least EUR 40 fixed compensation. For a debt that became late between 1 July 2026 and 31 December 2026, the minimum in euro countries that use the ECB rate is 10,4% per year.

The rule in three parts

  • Rate. At least the reference rate plus 8 percentage points per year (Directive 2011/7/EU, Art. 2(6)). In euro countries the reference rate is the ECB main refinancing rate in force on 1 January (for debts that become late in the first half-year) or on 1 July (second half-year).
  • Fixed compensation. At least EUR 40 per late invoice for recovery costs (Directive 2011/7/EU, Article 6).
  • No reminder needed. Both can be claimed without sending a reminder first. Each country writes the rule into its own law, and some go above the minimum.

Example

An invoice of EUR 2.400,00 became late between 1 July 2026 and 31 December 2026 in a country that uses the EU minimum. After 30 days the interest is EUR 2.400,00 x 10,4% / 365 x 30 = EUR 20,52. With the EUR 40 compensation you can claim EUR 2.460,52.

Questions

What is the statutory late payment interest rate in the EU?

The EU minimum is the reference rate plus 8 percentage points (Directive 2011/7/EU). In euro countries that use the ECB rate, the reference rate for a debt that became late between 1 July 2026 and 31 December 2026 is 2,4%, so the minimum is 10,4% per year. Some countries add more, for example Germany adds 9 points to its own base rate.

Do I have to send a reminder before I can claim interest?

No. Under the directive, interest and the fixed compensation of at least EUR 40 can be claimed on a late business invoice without sending a reminder first.

Does this apply to private customers?

No. These rules cover business-to-business invoices. They do not apply to private consumers.
